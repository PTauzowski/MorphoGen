What the fillet needs to blend:

In generateSegment2a, the bevel-end axial coordinate is:

At t=1 this is a planar elliptical cut. Segment 2b mirrors it. On the outer wall (r_node=R) the two outer cylinders meet at an included angle of 180° − 2α = 135° — a convex ridge. That is exactly where the diagnostic put the peak. A toroidal fillet of radius ρ must replace that ridge.

Two implementation options:

Option A — Node morphing (post-process, no topology change)
After each mesh1.merge(...) call in geterateManipulator, identify outer-wall nodes within distance ρ of the ridge line and project them onto the toroidal surface. No new elements, no topology change, minimal code. Works cleanly as long as ρ < ~0.5·wall thickness (otherwise elements invert near the inner radius). For wall = R − r = 4 mm, safe range is ρ ≤ 2 mm.

Option B — Fillet ring elements (exact geometry)
Insert a thin toroidal mesh patch between each pair of half-segments at the joint. The patch is resCirc × resTh × nFillet hexahedral elements on a torus of radius ρ. Exact geometry, no element distortion, but requires matching node counts at both interfaces and increases element count by ~resCirc × resTh per joint.

Recommendation: Option A first, Option B if ρ needs to be larger than ~2 mm. For the thin-wall arm (wall = 4 mm), Option A with ρ = 1–2 mm is both safe and sufficient to significantly reduce Kt.
